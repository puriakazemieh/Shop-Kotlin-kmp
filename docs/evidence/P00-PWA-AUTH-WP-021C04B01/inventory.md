## Schema, equality and safety requirements

- The inspected WP 6.6.2 image's `wp-admin/includes/schema.php:191–236` declares ordinary, not unique, indexes for `user_login`, `user_email` and `usermeta.meta_key`. The earlier controlled two-process test reproduced duplicate login/email on three actual WP cores. This says nothing about whether either persistent site contains duplicates.
- WP core sanitizes identifiers in its own flow; Bridge uses `sanitize_email` and `trim`, whereas the isolated browser helper uses SQL equality under the actual table collation. It does not establish equivalence of Persian/Arabic digit forms, prefixes, whitespace inside phone numbers, Unicode, case/accent rules or canonical email semantics. B02 must explicitly choose supported canonicalization and collisions, and prove it aligns with lookup/uniqueness and migration.
- A private lock or separate claim table used solely by the future browser route cannot force WP admin, WooCommerce, legacy API, imports, CLI or unknown third parties to participate. Post-checks alone cannot retroactively make all concurrent writers atomic. B02 must state a verifiable support boundary and fail-closed activation conditions, including what happens when a writer bypasses enforcement or a conflict is introduced after activation.
- Design must cover login/email/mobile cross-namespace collisions, multiple meta rows, ambiguous existing identities, account ownership, password-change/session races, user creation followed by failed session creation, policy/filter decisions and non-destructive reconciliation. A billing contact number must not automatically acquire login/OTP authority.
- No database uniqueness constraint, schema modification, migration or toggle should be added to persistent sites in this task. Existing mobile endpoint compatibility and future independent Theme/Plugin packaging remain separate acceptance requirements, not assumptions.
